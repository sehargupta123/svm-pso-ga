Phase 1

Frame the problem

An RBF SVM needs C and γ, and no formula gives them

Accuracy depends on both settings, and every guess costs a full training run.

detail
Why it's hard
Cross-validated accuracy moves in jumps of one patient, has no gradient and has broad flat regions, so calculus-based methods do not apply.
Question
With a fixed budget of evaluations, which search method finds good settings fastest and most reliably?

Use the Breast Cancer Wisconsin (Diagnostic) data

569 patients, 30 measurements each, malignant or benign.

detail
Why
Small, clean, no missing values, and built into scikit-learn, so every run is reproducible on a laptop. It is also the dataset of the reference GitHub repo, which gives a baseline.
Alternative
Harder UCI sets such as Sonar or Ionosphere. Kept as an option in data.py for future work.
Phase 2

Prepare the data

Relabel the classes as −1 and +1

Malignant = −1, benign = +1.

detail
Why
The SVM's constraints use y·f(x) ≥ 1, which only means "correct and confident" when labels are ±1.

Hold out a test set: 80/20, stratified, fixed seed

455 patients for tuning, 114 touched only once at the very end.

detail
Why
Tuning on the test set would make its score optimistic. Stratifying keeps the malignant share equal in both parts; the fixed seed makes results repeat exactly.

Scale the features?

Area is in the hundreds, smoothness is about 0.1.

YES standardise, fitted inside each CV foldNO distances dominated by a few features
detail
Evidence
A default SVM scored 93.0% unscaled and 98.2% scaled (Step 2).
Detail
The scaler is fitted on the training part of each fold only, so no information leaks from the validation part. The reference repo did not scale at all.
Phase 3

Build the model

Write the SVM ourselves: solve the dual quadratic programme

RBF kernel, cvxopt QP solver, optional SMO solver.

detail
Why
Training an SVM is a convex QP with Kuhn-Tucker optimality conditions, which ties the project to Units 1 and 2 of the syllabus. A library call would hide all of it.
Alternative
scikit-learn's SVC as a black box, as the reference repo does. Used only to validate and to speed up long experiments.

Does it match scikit-learn and pass the KKT check?

Compared at five (C, γ) settings on a validation split.

YES identical accuracy in 5 of 5, KKT 98.2–100%NO fix tolerances or bias, test again
detail
What happened
The loop back was used once: fixed tolerances under-reported optimality at large C (KKT pass rate 95.4% at C = 100). Making them relative to C brought every setting to 98–100%.

Use the RBF kernel

It gives the tuners a real two-dimensional search over C and γ.

detail
Why
The standard default kernel; flexible from nearly linear (small γ) to very curved (large γ).
Later check
Step 9 found a linear kernel matches RBF at 98.02%, so the data are nearly linearly separable. A useful limitation to report.
Phase 4

Define the search

Score a guess by 5-fold cross-validated accuracy

One evaluation trains the SVM five times.

detail
Why
Accuracy on the training data would reward memorising. CV estimates accuracy on unseen patients.
Tie-break
Subtract 10−6 per support vector, at most 0.00036, smaller than one patient (0.0022). It only picks the simpler model among exact ties.

Search log10C in [−2, 3] and log10γ in [−4, 1]

C from 0.01 to 1000, γ from 0.0001 to 10.

detail
Why log
Both settings matter by order of magnitude, so equal steps in log space mean equal effects.
Why these limits
At γ = 10−4 every patient looks alike (kernel ≈ 0.996); at γ = 10 every patient is unique and accuracy falls to 62.6%. C = 0.01 barely fits; by C = 1000 accuracy is flat.

Is the best point stuck on an edge where accuracy is still rising?

Checked on a 60 × 60 accuracy map.

NO keep the boxYES widen the box, search again
detail
Evidence
Three corners sit at the 62.6% floor and the fourth is already flat; the best settings found lie well inside. Only about 7% of the box reaches 97.5%, so where you search still matters.
Phase 5

Choose and run the tuners

Main methods: PSO and GA. Baselines: grid, random, TPE, SA

PSO and GA are written from scratch; TPE comes from Optuna.

detail
Why
PSO and GA are the Unit 5 metaheuristics in the title. Grid and random search are the classical baselines; TPE is the modern one used in industry; SA is the third Unit 5 method and a single-point search.

Make the comparison fair

100 evaluations each, the same folds, independent random seeds.

detail
GA fix
The first GA re-scored repeated points and wasted about 49 of its 100 evaluations. It now nudges any repeat to a new point.
Seeds
Each method gets its own random stream, so runs are independent, which the significance test assumes.

Repeat each stochastic method 30 times

151 runs and 15,100 evaluations in total.

detail
Why
One run can be lucky or unlucky. In the single seed-0 run, PSO needed 76 trials; over 30 runs its median was 34.5.
Phase 6

Measure and conclude

Does final accuracy separate the methods?

Every method ends near 98% test accuracy.

NO compare speed and reliability insteadYES rank by accuracy
detail
Evidence
Mean test accuracy 97.8–98.25%; every final model misses the same 1 of 42 cancers.
New measures
Trials to reach 97.92% CV accuracy (the top level, 446 of 455 patients), and the share of the 30 runs that ever get there.

Are the speed differences real?

Mann-Whitney U test on trials to target.

YES PSO, GA, SA, TPE beat random (p ≤ .02)NO PSO vs GA (p = .394)
detail
Why this test
Trial counts are not normally distributed, so a rank-based test is used. Runs that never reach the target count as 101 trials.

Conclusion: metaheuristics save evaluations, but neither PSO nor GA wins outright

PSO reached the top level in 28/30 runs, the GA in 22/30, random search in 15/30.

detail
Speed
Median trials when successful: GA 31, PSO 34.5, SA 36.5, TPE 39, random 59, grid 63.
Most reliable
TPE reached the top level in all 30 runs.
Why it's not universal
The search surface has one broad good region, so the ranking reflects this problem. That is the No Free Lunch theorem in practice.

Extensions run after the main study: a sensitivity check of PSO and GA settings, a GA search over kernel types, and an NSGA-II search for the accuracy versus model size trade-off.

Inside each tuner

How each method picks its next trial

Every method repeats its loop until 100 evaluations are used. Dashed boxes run once at the start; the coloured arrow on the right is the loop back. Results are from the 30-run experiment.

PSO

main method
  1. Place 10 particles at random, with random velocities
  2. New velocity = keep going + pull to own best + pull to swarm best
  3. Move each particle and keep it inside the box
  4. Score all 10 positions
  5. Better than own best or swarm best? Update the memory
reached 28/30median 34.5 trials

Genetic algorithm

main method
  1. Score a random population of 10
  2. Keep the best 2 unchanged
  3. Pick 2 parents: best of 3 random individuals, twice
  4. Blend them (80%), maybe mutate (15%)
  5. Already tried? Nudge to a new point, then score
reached 22/30median 31 trials

Bayesian (TPE)

baseline
  1. Score a few random points to start
  2. Split past trials into a good group and a bad group
  3. Model where good trials cluster and where bad ones do
  4. Choose the point where good most outweighs bad
  5. Score it and add it to the history
reached 30/30median 39 trials

Simulated annealing

baseline
  1. Start at one random point, temperature 0.02
  2. Propose a random nearby point
  3. Score it
  4. Better? Accept. Worse? Accept with a chance that shrinks as it cools
  5. Cool the temperature by 5%
reached 24/30median 36.5 trials

Random search

baseline
  1. Draw a point uniformly in the log box
  2. Score it
  3. Best so far? Remember it

Never uses what it has learned, so it cannot refine around a good find.

reached 15/30median 59 trials

Grid search

baseline
  1. Lay out a 10 × 10 grid of log-spaced points
  2. Take the next point, row by row from small C
  3. Score it
  4. Best so far? Remember it

Deterministic: one run only. Its order decides when it reaches the good region.

reached 1/1found at trial 63
